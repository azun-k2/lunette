What it does

Lunette draws tonight's Moon in the menu bar at its real phase, with the next sunrise or sunset beside it. Click it and the whole sky for the day opens up.

  • The Moon as it looks tonight: its phase, how much of it is lit, how far away it is, and when it rises and sets where you are.
  • The day at a glance: a chart of the Sun's arc over the horizon, coloured by what the sky is doing — night, twilight, blue hour, golden hour, day — with the Moon's path dotted across it.
  • Sunrise, sunset and the length of the day, and how much longer or shorter it is than yesterday.
  • Golden hour and blue hour, morning and evening, for photographers. Civil, nautical and astronomical twilight too, if you want them.
  • The next four phases: first quarter, full moon, last quarter and new moon, to the minute.

Requires macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

Worked out on your Mac

Every figure comes from the formulas astronomers publish, calculated on your Mac from the date and your location. There is no weather service behind it, no download and nothing to go out of date, and the times agree with the US Naval Observatory's almanac to within a minute or two.

Seen from south of the equator the Moon appears upside down and lit from the other side. Lunette draws it the way it looks from where you are.

Privacy in one paragraph

Lunette asks for your location only to work out sunrise, sunset and moonrise where you are. It keeps it rounded to about a kilometre, on your Mac, and has no network access to send it anywhere. There is no account, no analytics and no tracking. If you would rather not share your location at all, Lunette uses your time zone's main city instead, which is usually within a few minutes. The full privacy policy says the same at greater length.
