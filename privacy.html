The short version

Lunette uses your location to work out the Sun and Moon times where you are. The location stays on your Mac. The app has no network access, collects nothing and sends nothing.

Your location

macOS asks for your permission before Lunette may use your location. If you allow it, Lunette asks Location Services for your position when it starts and every few hours after that — it never tracks you continuously — and rounds it to two decimal places, about a kilometre, before keeping it. That rounded position is used for one thing: calculating sunrise, sunset, twilight, golden hour, moonrise and moonset.

The name of your town, when macOS can supply one, is shown in the panel and kept alongside the position.

If you decline, or turn Location Services off, Lunette uses the main city of your Mac's time zone instead. The Moon's phase is the same everywhere and needs no location at all.

Where it goes

Nowhere. Lunette has no network entitlement, which means macOS itself will not let it open a network connection. There is no server, no analytics library, no crash reporter and no account.

What is stored, and where

Inside the app's own sandbox container on your Mac: the rounded position and place name described above, and your display settings. Nothing else. Deleting the app removes its container and everything in it.

Email

The Email the developer button opens a message in your own mail app with the app version, your macOS version, the kind of Mac and how Lunette found your location (for example "Location Services" or "time zone city") filled in. It does not include your position. Nothing is sent unless you send the message yourself.

Children

Lunette collects no personal information from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the new version will appear on this page with a new date, and any change to what the app collects would also be described in the release notes.
