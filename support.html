Location

Lunette asks for your location the first time it starts. If the panel says the times are for your time zone's city, it does not have your location yet:

  • Click Allow location in the panel, or in Settings ▸ Location.
  • If you declined earlier, macOS will not ask again. Turn Lunette on in System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Location Services.
  • If Location Services are off for the whole Mac, the same page turns them on.

The time zone's city is usually within a few minutes, and the Moon's phase does not depend on where you are at all.

Common questions

The sunrise does not match what I see

Rise and set times are for a flat, open horizon at sea level, the way every almanac gives them. Hills, buildings and the weather can move what you see by a few minutes, and the atmosphere itself varies from one evening to the next. Lunette's figures agree with the US Naval Observatory's to within a minute or two.

There is no moonrise today

The Moon rises about fifty minutes later each day, so roughly once a month it skips a calendar day altogether. That day really has no moonrise, and Lunette says None today.

What exactly is golden hour?

Lunette uses the definition most photography planners use: the Sun between 4° below the horizon and 6° above it. Blue hour is the Sun between 6° and 4° below. Near the equator both are short; far north or south they can last much longer, or not happen at all in midsummer.

The Moon is drawn the wrong way round

South of the equator a waxing Moon is lit on the left, not the right. Lunette decides from your latitude; you can choose a hemisphere yourself in Settings ▸ Panel.

When is it called a full moon?

Full, new and the two quarters are instants, given to the minute in Coming up. Like almanacs, Lunette uses the name for about half a day either side of that instant; the rest of the time the Moon is a crescent or gibbous.
